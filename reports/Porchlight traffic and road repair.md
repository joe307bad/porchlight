# Repair the roads, then teach cars manners

Porchlight should add a single topology repair and classification stage, called here **RoadSense**, to the generator. It should sit between `StreetsStage` and `BlocksStage` in `Gen/Generator.cs`. Downstream of it, Porchlight should build an explicit **junction model** in Shared/Explore: legs, movements, `ulong` foe and yield masks, stop lines and a control type per node. Cars then ask that model for permission at the stop line, pedestrians reserve crossings, and a sparse, owner-editable **TrafficConfig** overrides the auto-assigned defaults. The design draws on SUMO's netconvert and junction model, OSMnx, osm2streets/A/B Street and TM:PE. All of them separate "clean the graph", "decide who has right of way" and "simulate agents". Porchlight's current code merges those three jobs into one hardcoded rule set: cars in `Explore/Traffic.cs` choose a random exit and never yield. Townsfolk are not even in the car obstacle list at `Game/App/ExploreView.cs` around line 1902, so cars drive through them today. The fixed scale `Directions.METRES_PER_TILE = 4` resolves most of the disagreements between the four research notes. At that scale, cars at `CRUISE = 2.4` tiles/s move at a realistic ~35 km/h, while walkers at 0.9–1.5 tiles/s move at 3.6–6 m/s, three to five times real walking pace. So **real-world vehicle numbers (yellow times, headways, join distances) convert directly, but real-world pedestrian timings do not**. Several primary sources were blocked during research: sumo.dlr.de, the FHWA/MUTCD sites, Wikipedia, arXiv and gdcvault. SUMO was read from its GitHub Markdown and C++ source instead. US engineering values come from state DOT and agency summaries, and the car-following equations are standard published forms written from memory. Every number below is labelled as either sourced or a tuning guess.

## One scale fact settles most disagreements: cars are real-speed, walkers are 3–5× fast

The research notes were written independently, and only the road-repair note confirmed the scale in code: `public const double METRES_PER_TILE = 4` in `Explore/Directions.cs`. The pedestrian note guessed "about 4 m" but listed the scale as undefined in its gaps. The signals note says outright that "the game's tile-to-metre scale ... [was] not determined" and reasoned its timings from `CRUISE`, `GAP` and `CORNER` alone. At 4 m per tile, Main asphalt (`mainRoadWidth = 2`) is 8 m and Streets are 4 m. The current cruise of 2.4 tiles/s is 9.6 m/s, about 35 km/h, which is a believable small-town speed. Pedestrian pace in `Actors.cs` (`rng.Range(0.9, 1.5)`) works out to 3.6–6.0 m/s. Published design walking speeds are 1.07 m/s (MUTCD 2009's 3.5 ft/s), with 0.9 m/s for older pedestrians and 1.2 m/s recommended by FHWA ([NAP TCRP/NCHRP](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/13962/chapter/10); [FHWA-RD-98-107](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/pedbike/98107/section2.cfm)). SUMO's default pedestrian speed is 1.39 m/s ([SUMO vType defaults](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Vehicle_Type_Parameter_Defaults.md)).

That asymmetry corrects one claim in the pedestrian note. It said 0.9–1.5 m/s walking speeds "match Porchlight's 0.9–1.5 pace range", but the match holds only if game units are taken as metres. They are tiles. Porchlight's walkers are deliberately quick, and the car-to-walker speed ratio is about 2× rather than the real ~10×. The practical consequence is a split rule. Kinematic vehicle quantities convert from metres to tiles at ÷4 and can be used almost as published. Pedestrian timing must be rebuilt from game pace and expressed as time-to-arrival in sim seconds, never as a borrowed distance or duration. The signals note also worried that light cycles might be "time-compressed". They are not, because cars are not. Short cycles are still right, but the reason is Webster's formula at low volume (see below), not a scale factor.

The current car kinematics are the other scale surprise. `Traffic.cs` eases speed with `speed += (target - speed)·min(1, 3dt)`, so a car at cruise first brakes at about 7.2 tiles/s². That is roughly 29 m/s², or 3 g. The jerky stops come from this lag, not from the scale, and it is a reason to replace the ramp with a bounded model later in the roadmap.

## RoadSense: a fixed-point repair stage between Streets and Blocks

The mature network tools agree on a core set of operations ([SUMO NBNodeCont.cpp](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/src/netbuild/NBNodeCont.cpp); [OSMnx simplification.py](https://github.com/gboeing/osmnx/blob/main/osmnx/simplification.py); [osm2streets transforms](https://github.com/a-b-street/osm2streets/blob/main/osm2streets/src/transform/mod.rs)). They cluster nearby junctions and rebuild one node per cluster. They collapse very short edges, fold degree-2 "geometry" nodes into polylines, drop disconnected components, and only then guess control from road rank. Each tool adds guard rules. SUMO rejects clusters with more than 5 incoming edges or with external legs less than 30° apart. OSMnx splits a spatial cluster into its connected sub-components, so a bridge over a street never fuses with it. osm2streets never collapses a road that touches a map-edge node, and re-runs collapse after degenerate-node merging because the first pass "may discover more roads to collapse". The procedural-generation literature mostly enforces validity *during* growth through Parish & Müller-style local constraints ([SIGGRAPH 2001](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=116923); [constraint summary](https://tfdml.blogspot.com/2015/03/procedural-city-generation.html)). Porchlight's `StreetsStage` already does this, with `MIN_ANGLE` 35°, `JUNCTION_CLEARANCE` 3, `DUPLICATE_DISTANCE` 1.5 and the snapping rules. The defects that remain come from stages interacting. `RoadsStage` produces RDP-simplified 8-connected A* mains at 45°, grid-snapped streets are seeded on them, and connectors and footpaths are added after planarisation. That argues for one post-pass rather than more rules inside each stage.

RoadSense should be a pure `IStage` that slots between `StreetsStage` and `BlocksStage` in `Generator.Stages`. That position comes after `KeepConnected` and the footpaths, and before blocks and lots are cut from road polygons, so every repair reshapes blocks consistently. It needs no RNG. If it ever does, `Rng.Stage(seed, "roadsense")` gives it an isolated stream, and the per-stage streams mean inserting it does not perturb other stages' random draws. Blocks and lots will still change wherever geometry is repaired, so the stage must sit behind a generator version flag. Towns already saved through the `towns.layout` column must not silently reshape. It should reuse `RoadGraphOps` from `Gen/RoadGraph.cs` (`Planarize`, `MergePassThrough(keep)`, `SplitEdgeAt`, `Compact`, `ComputeBridges`/`CheckBridges`, `ReachableNodes`) and the checks in `Gen/Streets.cs` (`CheckCrossings`, `CheckDuplicates`, `LandOk`).

The operations run in this order, and the whole sequence loops to a fixed point with a cap of 8 passes:

1. Protect gates, the airport and zoo road ends, and the centre hub. This is the existing `keep` set and the osm2streets map-edge rule.
2. Join degree-≥3 junctions that lie within **2.5 tiles along an edge**, splitting each cluster into its connected sub-clusters. Reject a merge that would give more than 4 legs or two legs under 30° apart. Merge to the most important member's position rather than a drifting centroid.
3. Collapse edges shorter than 2.5 tiles (Streets) or 3.5 tiles (Mains) between two junctions.
4. Merge pass-through nodes only where `RoadKind` matches.
5. Repair dog-legs. Opposite-side T's within 2.5 tiles slide together into a 4-way. Those between 2.5 and 9.5 tiles (15 on mains) slide if the stem's far end is free; otherwise they are flagged for stop control on both stems.
6. Realign legs that meet below **60°**, and warn for 60–75°. AASHTO accepts 60–120°, while NCHRP 279 and ITE prefer at least 75° ([Iowa DOT 6A-8](https://iowadot.gov/design-manual/chapter-6-geometric-design/6a-8-intersection-alignment-and-realignment); [FHWA Older Road User Handbook](https://highways.dot.gov/safety/other/older-road-user/desk-reference-handbook-designing-roadways-aging-population/chapter-2)).
7. Keep junctions at least 2 tiles from bridge ends (a guess: the corner curve plus a car length).
8. Remove short stubs and tiny all-Street loops.
9. Re-assert the centre component and run a final `MergePassThrough` → `Planarize` → `Compact` before looping.

Every geometric move must re-pass `Raster.RoadSegmentOk`/`LandOk`. The road-repair note did not verify that moves keep slopes legal.

**The junction-merge threshold is the main contradiction to resolve.** The signals note proposed a union-find merge at about 1.5 tiles inside a runtime `Junctions` build step and called that "like SUMO's `junctions.join` at 10 m". It did not know the scale, though, and 1.5 tiles is only 6 m. The road-repair note proposed 2.5 tiles, the actual SUMO 10 m default, inside the generator. The repair belongs in the generator, at 2.5 tiles. Merging changes topology and block shapes, so doing it at runtime would leave Layout, sidewalks and blocks describing a different graph from the one cars drive. The runtime junction builder should then *assert* that no two degree-≥3 nodes remain within 2.5 tiles. Separately, it may group nodes within 5 tiles (SUMO's 20 m `tls.join`) into one *signal controller* without changing geometry. Two caveats apply. SUMO's join default was 15 m in older releases ([Ubuntu man page](https://manpages.ubuntu.com/manpages/bionic/man1/netconvert.1.html)), so "10 m" is a convention rather than a law. And OSMnx's "10 m" means a 5 m buffer *radius*, or 1.25 tiles. The value should be confirmed by histogramming junction-pair distances over many seeds before and after the stage. Streets' 3-tile `JUNCTION_CLEARANCE` suggests few legitimate junctions sit inside 2.5 tiles.

After repair, RoadSense classifies roads and assigns control, because control depends on final topology. SUMO's TLS guessing "assumes nodes are already joined" ([NBNodeCont.cpp](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/src/netbuild/NBNodeCont.cpp)). Classification combines strokes (best-continuation chains under about 30° deflection, after COINS and Thomson ([Tripathy et al.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2399808320967680); [Thomson](https://www.spacesyntax.net/symposia-archive/SSS4/fullpapers/50Thomsonfpaper.pdf))) with Brandes edge betweenness, using node-id tie-breaks. A Main on any gate→centre shortest path is an **arterial**. Other Mains, plus top-quintile Street strokes linking two Mains, are **collectors**. Graph-bridge branches to dead ends are **cul-de-sacs**, and everything else is **local**. The quintile cut-offs and the 30° threshold are design choices, since no source gave either. This is needed because `RoadsStage` emits every edge as `RoadKind.Main`, so "Main" alone cannot tell a through route from a bypass. TM:PE's width-based main-road detection is documented to "get confused" when widths are equal ([TM:PE wiki](https://github.com/CitiesSkylinesMods/TMPE/wiki/High-Priority-Roads)). Default class speeds are 50/40/30/20 km/h, which is 3.5/2.8/2.1/1.4 tiles/s. Those are game defaults, not standards. Today's 2.4 tiles/s sits between collector and local. The model gains `RoadEdge.cls/speed/lanesFwd/lanesBack/priority` and `RoadNode.control/stopLegs`. `kind` stays as physical width, and `cls` holds functional class.

## Junction control: one decision table reconciling three heuristics

The notes proposed three overlapping control rules. The signals note signalised any node with at least 2 Main legs plus a Street, or at least 4 legs with any Main, within a budget of 1–4 lights. The road-repair note required at least 3 arterial or collector legs, at least 2 of them arterial, a SUMO-style score S = Σ lanes × class speed (m/s) ≥ 45, at least 5 tiles from another signal, and a cap of about one light per 60 tiles of arterial. The management note left control on "Auto". The road-repair version should win, because it runs on classified roads rather than raw `RoadKind`. Every generated arterial is a Main, so the signals note's rule would light up most of the centre. Its structure follows SUMO's `shouldBeTLSControlled`, which compares a summed incoming lanes × speed against 69.44 m/s, with a "too fast" cutoff at 79 km/h ([NBNodeCont.cpp](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/src/netbuild/NBNodeCont.cpp)). That threshold matters. With one lane each way, a 4-way 50 km/h crossing scores only 55.6, so SUMO itself would never signal Porchlight's busiest junction, and a game threshold of 45 is a deliberate guess. Note also that with S ≥ 45, a 3-leg arterial T (41.7) never gets a light. That is acceptable for a small town but worth knowing when tuning.

|---|---|---|
| Degree 1 | DeadEnd (Gate if protected) | existing U-turn code |
| Degree 2 | None | SUMO treats continuations as non-junctions ([NBAlgorithms.cpp](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/src/netbuild/NBAlgorithms.cpp)) |
| ≥3 arterial/collector legs, ≥2 arterial, S ≥ 45, budget allows | Signal | SUMO Σ(lanes×speed) structure; threshold and budget are guesses |

| All legs same class (local/collector), 4+ legs | AllWayStop (US flavour) or RightBeforeLeft (European flavour), chosen per town seed | A/B Street "same rank → all-way stop" ([stop_signs.rs](https://github.com/a-b-street/abstreet/blob/main/map_model/src/objects/stop_signs.rs)); SUMO uses right-before-left only below 49 km/h, which all locals satisfy |
| Local T | Stem yields | A/B Street / TM:PE |

US warrants support the spirit but not the numbers. MUTCD's multi-way stop warrant asks for about 300 veh/h major and 200 units/h minor for 8 hours ([PennDOT TS-213](https://gis.penndot.pa.gov/BPR_PDF_FILES/Documents/LTAP/TechSheets/TS_213_Multiway_Stop_Control.pdf)), and signal warrants are volume tables ([2009 MUTCD Ch. 4C archive](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20130225063604/http:/mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part4/part4c.htm)). Porchlight has no measured volumes at generation time, so class and betweenness stand in for them. Once origin–destination demand exists (Phase 6), the same table could be re-run on simulated volumes.

At runtime, `Layout.Build` copies class, speed and control into `RoadStroke`. A new `Junctions.Build(IsoLayout)` in Shared/Explore then turns each node into a `Junction`. One leg per road end, sorted by angle. One movement per ordered leg pair, classified with SUMO's angle thresholds (under 44° is straight; over 90° is a left or right turn; reversed is a U-turn) ([NBNode.cpp](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/src/netbuild/NBNode.cpp)). A 5-leg junction has at most 20 movements, so `ulong` masks fit. Foes come from SUMO's topological "chords interleave on the cyclic leg order" test plus merges into the same exit ([NBRequest.cpp](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/src/netbuild/NBRequest.cpp)). The *location* of each conflict comes from intersecting the Bézier corner curves `Traffic.Place` already draws, as CityFlow's `initCrosses` does ([roadnet.cpp](https://github.com/cityflow-project/CityFlow/blob/master/src/roadnet/roadnet.cpp)). Yield bits follow SUMO's `setBlocking` order: U-turn yields; the lower-priority leg yields; a turn yields to straight traffic; otherwise right-before-left. The builder asserts the relation is antisymmetric, because mutual yielding is a known deadlock source.

For simulation, car-side permission is a single call. Choose `car.next` earlier than today: Traffic.cs picks it when `rest < max(CORNER, TurnReach)`, and it should pick at roughly stop-line plus braking distance, about 3 tiles out. Then call `junction.MayEnter(car, movement, simMs)` and, if refused, place a virtual stationary leader at the stop line. Inside `MayEnter`, a car already in the box always proceeds, which is CityFlow's "cannot yield" rule. Red refuses. Yellow proceeds only if the car cannot stop comfortably, and that decision is cached per car. Stop control needs a dwell of about 0.5–1 s at under 0.1 tiles/s. All-way stop serves the lowest `(arrivalTick, carIndex)` among stopped foes, matching SUMO's "conflict resolution happens via arrival time". Permitted and priority movements check that no foe is in the box and that no higher-priority car arrives within `ownCrossTime + 1 s`, SUMO's uncalibrated `jmTimegapMinor` default ([sumo-user list](https://www.eclipse.org/lists/sumo-user/msg03919.html)). Every entry also passes a keep-clear test: the exit must have `GAP` plus a car length free. That is SUMO's default no-block heuristic ([SUMO Intersections](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Simulation/Intersections.html)), and it needs one O(cars) "rear-most car per road direction" cache per tick, which also lets `GapAhead` see queues across the junction. The `PATIENCE` U-turn becomes a last resort: re-pick an exit with room, and never time out a red light.

## Signals: short fixed-time cycles derived from the shared clock

SUMO's model fits Porchlight directly. A light is a list of phases, each a per-*movement* state string, and the controller state is just (program, phase, time in phase) ([SUMO Traffic Lights](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Simulation/Traffic_Lights.html)). Porchlight needs only Red, Yellow, protected Green, permitted green (go but yield to foes), FlashYellow, FlashRed and Off. Plans are generated with netconvert's recipe from `NBOwnTLDef::computeLogicAndConts` ([source](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/src/netbuild/NBOwnTLDef.cpp)). Greedily pick the highest-priority, most-opposite leg pair, give its movements G, add every compatible movement, and downgrade lefts that conflict with oncoming straight traffic to permitted g. Porchlight has no turn lanes, and SUMO itself only builds protected-left phases with a dedicated lane. A 4-leg junction yields 2 stages, a Main-through T 2 stages, and a 5-leg junction 3. The generator asserts `(greenMask & foes[m]) == 0` for every protected movement.

Timing is where the notes disagreed most. The road-repair note proposed SUMO's 90 s cycle and 31 s greens "scaled to game time". The signals note proposed 24–40 s cycles with a 2.5 s yellow, both justified by an assumed compression. The scale fact changes the justification but supports the shorter cycle:

- **Yellow.** The ITE kinematic yellow `t + v/2a`, with t = 1 s and a = 10 ft/s² (3.05 m/s²) ([NJDOT appendix](https://www.nj.gov/transportation/about/publicat/lmreports/pdf/rlrappendix11.2011.pdf); [ITE](https://ite.org/technical-resources/topics/traffic-engineering/traffic-signal-change-and-clearance-intervals)), gives 1 + 9.6/6.1 ≈ **2.6 s** at Porchlight's real 9.6 m/s. SUMO's German rule gives 3 s for speeds up to 50 km/h ([NBTrafficLightDefinition.cpp](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/src/netbuild/NBTrafficLightDefinition.cpp)). Use **3 s**, now a converted value rather than a guess.
- **All-red.** `(W + L)/v`, with a junction crossing of about 2 × `CORNER` = 7.2 m and a 4–6 m vehicle, gives 1.2–1.4 s. Use 1–1.5 s.
- **Minimum green.** 5 s, the floor in both FHWA guidance and SUMO's `tls.min-dur` ([netconvert docs](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/netconvert.html)).
- **Cycle length.** Webster's cycle `C₀ = (1.5L + 5)/(1 − Y)` ([U. Idaho NIATT](https://www.webpages.uidaho.edu/niatt_labmanual/Chapters/signaltimingdesign/theoryandconcepts/CycleLengthDetermination.htm)) falls to about 17 s as flow ratios Y approach zero, with two stages of 4 s lost time. Seventy cars spread over a town is the near-zero-flow case. So a **30–45 s** cycle (greens 10–15 s) is the efficient regime for this traffic, not a compromise for player patience. Real 60–120 s cycles reflect real volumes, and no primary source for that range was retrieved. The specific 10–15 s greens are tuning guesses.

Fixed-time state is a pure function, `phase = f((simMs + offsetMs) mod cycleMs)` in integer milliseconds of the *synced sim clock*, not the accelerated day/night clock. Clients can then compute every lamp locally and the host sends nothing. The notes also disagreed on offsets: a per-node id hash versus a green wave. Use a green wave along the arterial stroke, with offset = distance from the first signal ÷ class speed, following SUMO's `tlsCoordinator` idea ([SUMO tls tools](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Tools/tls.html)). Use the deterministic id hash only for unconnected lights. Gap-actuated control is an optional later mode: minGreen 5 s and maxGreen about 20 s, ending green after a 2–3 s gap with demand waiting, after SUMO's `max-gap` 3 s and TM:PE's wait-versus-flow metric ([TimedTrafficLightsStep.cs](https://github.com/CitiesSkylinesMods/TMPE/blob/master/TLM/TLM/TrafficLight/Impl/TimedTrafficLightsStep.cs)). It costs three small integers per signal in the 10 Hz `WorldSync` stream. Late-night flash, with yellow on the major road and red on the minor, cut total delay by about 50% at low volume in USDOT research. FDOT allows it below 200 veh/h two-way ([ROSA P low-volume guidelines](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/16200); [FDOT TEM ch. 3](https://www.fdot.gov/docs/default-source/traffic/trafficservices/Studies/TEM/TEM2017/TEM-Chapter-3-June-2018.pdf)). It maps onto the existing day/night and glowing-bulb systems, with flash red treated as a stop sign. Rendering is one pole and head per incoming leg, whose colour is `Junction.HeadLight(leg, simMs)`, the most restrictive state among that leg's movements, set per frame through a `MaterialPropertyBlock`. Stop signs and stop bars are static props. No source was found for flasher rate, so ~1 Hz is a guess.

Pedestrian phases need game-scale arithmetic. MUTCD-derived practice is WALK of at least 7 s (4 s at low volume), clearance computed at 3.5 ft/s, and a leading pedestrian interval of at least 3 s ([NCDOT LPI](https://connect.ncdot.gov/resources/safety/Teppl/TEPPL%20All%20Documents%20Library/NCDOT%20Leading%20Pedestrian%20Interval%20(LPI)%20Guidance.pdf); [FHWA LPI](https://highways.dot.gov/safety/proven-safety-countermeasures/leading-pedestrian-interval)). Because walkers are 3–5× fast, clearance should be computed as kerb-to-kerb width over the slowest game pace. Crossing a Main (2 tiles of asphalt plus 0.5-tile parking each side) at 0.9 tiles/s takes about 3.3 s. SUMO's 4 s minimum and 5 s clearance defaults are a reasonable round-up. A 1–2 s leading interval preserves the LPI idea at game scale (a guess).

## Pedestrians become virtual leaders, and crossings become reservable

The cheapest high-impact fix is the obstacle list. In `ExploreView.cs` (lines ~1898–1907), the host builds `obstacles` from traffic cars, the local player on foot, other players, `Obstacles` and parked cars. The `people` list of townsfolk updates on the line just before but is never added, so `traffic.Update` cannot see them. Two further limits apply. `BlockedAhead` in `Traffic.cs` projects obstacles along `car.heading` within 4 tiles (`GAP * 2.5`) and `LANE_HALF` 0.42, so it misses anyone around a corner curve. And mid-block crossings in `Pedestrian.Update` (`rng.Chance(dt * 0.02)` → `walk.CrossStreet()` in `Walking.cs`) step into the road without looking. The interim fix is to append townsfolk who are on the carriageway to `obstacles`. The proper fix passes a `WalkerIntent {pos, vel, crossingId, phase}` list into `Traffic.Update`, built in Shared rather than in the Unity view so it stays engine-free and testable. Ideally the whole host step moves into a Shared orchestrator, so ordering is fixed and replayable.

The interaction model follows SUMO's crossing rules, translated through the scale split. A car may not enter a crossing that is occupied, or that has a pedestrian walking toward the conflict point within `jmCrossingGap`, 10 m or **2.5 tiles**. Pedestrians start an unprioritised crossing only if it is clear "for the whole time needed to cross". Impatience grows with waiting, `clamp(base + wait/120 s)` for pedestrians and wait/180 s for drivers. A walker jammed on a crossing for 10 s pushes through ([SUMO Pedestrians](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Simulation/Pedestrians.md); [SUMO vType junction params](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Definition_of_Vehicles%2C_Vehicle_Types%2C_and_Routes.md)).

Walkers get a small state machine: Approach → Look (0.4–1.2 s, seeded) → Wait → Cross → Done. The gap test is computed entirely in game time: accept when every approaching car's `tta = dist/max(v, 0.3)` exceeds `t_cross + margin`. Here `t_cross` is road width over the walker's own pace (0.7–1.1 s for a Street, 1.3–2.2 s for a Main), and the margin shrinks from 1.5 s to 0.5 s over about 15 s of waiting. Real critical gaps of 3.6–4.3 s on arterials ([IITH study](https://publications.iith.ac.in/publication/critical-gap-estimation-for-pedestrians-at-uncontrolled-mid-block-1)) and the HCM's `L/Sp + ts` with ts ≈ 3 s confirm the structure, crossing time plus a margin. Their absolute values do not transfer, because real crossing time is most of that gap and Porchlight's crossing time is a third of it. The margin values are tuning guesses.

Drivers roll a yield decision once per encounter, seeded and stored rather than re-rolled per frame. About 0.85 at junction mouths and 0.4 mid-block is a tuning choice anchored only in direction by FHWA findings: yielding is more common at marked junctions than at unmarked mid-block sites, and falls with speed ([FHWA-HRT-13-098](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/13098/001.cfm); [FHWA 10-043](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/pedbike/10043/003.cfm)). The FHWA figure that 8.93% of 70,000+ crossings involved a vehicle yielding describes *encounters*, not yield probability, and should not be used as one.

A two-phase reservation makes this deadlock-free. Each crossing holds `committed`, `occupants` and `carsCommitted` sets. A walker who passes the gap test commits. A car inside its own braking distance `v²/2b + 0.3` of an uncommitted crossing commits too, and waiting walkers treat it as unyielding. That removes the "both wait, both go" flip-flop. Three further guards complete it. Pedestrian blockers do not count toward `car.wait`, so cars never U-turn for walkers. After a car has waited about 6 s, a priority token makes uncommitted walkers let it pass. And a crossing that has had walkers on it for 8 s straight stops accepting new commitments. The last guard addresses the "continuous blockade of people crossing" that Cities: Skylines players report ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/cities-skylines-2/fix-pedestrians-mod); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/255710/discussions/0/3781371514573567813)). All three timers are guesses.

Placement reconciles three slightly different offsets in the notes (0.5–0.8, ~1, and stop line at `reach` plus half the cross-road width). From the node outward: the junction box out to `CORNER` (0.9 tiles), then the crosswalk centred just beyond it at about 1–1.2 tiles, aligned to the existing `SidewalkNetwork` junction-mouth links (`net.crossings`, `JUNCTION_CROSSING_COST = 2`), then the stop line 0.25 tile (SUMO's 1 m) before the crosswalk's near edge. The road-repair note's suggestion to drop crosswalks across priority roads, copying TM:PE's default, should not be taken literally. Removing sidewalk links would reroute every walker. Keep the links, and treat crossing the priority road as *unmarked*: lower driver yield, stricter walker margin. Mark crossings on minor, stop and signal legs. Mid-block crossings become transient crossing objects at the walker's arc position, ideally limited to straight segments at least 3 tiles from a junction.

The longitudinal model should move from the linear GAP ramp to the Intelligent Driver Model in a later phase. IDM gives a smooth, collision-free approach to any stationary virtual leader, whether stop line, red light or committed walker ([Kesting/Treiber/Helbing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0912.3613)). It is `a[1 − (v/v0)⁴ − (s*/s)²]` with `s* = s0 + vT + vΔv/2√(ab)`, the standard form written from memory, integrated ballistically with v clamped at 0. The signals note's alternative is to just feed `min(ahead, distToStop)` into the existing ramp. That is a fine Phase 2 shortcut, but it keeps the 3 g braking.

On parameters, the pedestrian note proposed a = 2 tiles/s² and b = 3 tiles/s², which is 8 and 12 m/s². With the scale known, that is arcade-strong. A converted Krauss/IDM set is a ≈ 0.65 tiles/s² (2.6 m/s²), comfortable b ≈ 1.1 tiles/s² (4.5 m/s²) and emergency ≈ 2.25 tiles/s² (9 m/s²). That set gives a 2.6-tile stopping distance from cruise, which fits inside the existing 4-tile sensor. A middle ground of about 1 / 1.5 / 4 tiles/s² is a reasonable starting tune.

The pedestrian note also argued for T = 0.6–0.8 s "because the game is time-compressed". That reasoning fails for cars. A sourced headway is T ≈ 1.0–1.5 s ([SUMO Car-Following Models](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Car-Following-Models/index.md)). Today's 1.6-tile centre-to-centre `GAP` at 9.6 m/s is a headway of about 0.25 s bumper to bumper, so cars tailgate badly. Raising T to 0.8–1.0 s will visibly thin queues. That is a look-and-feel decision to playtest. Finally, `BlockedAhead` should sample the car's *future* lane, corner and U-turn path at 0.25-tile steps out to `v·T + v²/2b + s0`, instead of projecting along the heading. Per-car variety (v0 × N(1, 0.1), as with SUMO's speedDev, and T × U(0.8, 1.2)) is drawn once at spawn from the seeded `Rng`.

## Management mode: sparse overrides keyed to geometry, owned on the server

A management layer matters only when traffic has a purpose. Cars currently wander, choosing `next` randomly in `PickNext`, so closing a road just reshuffles randomness. SimCity 2013 showed that pure shortest-path routing is the opposite failure. Maxis acknowledged "unavoidable, illogical jams" and patched roads to become less attractive as they fill ([AusGamers](https://www.ausgamers.com/news/read/3282101/simcity-lead-designer-reveals-upcoming-changes-to-traffic-congestion-modders-discover-offline-capabilities-and-inflated-population); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/2013/03/14/simcity-traffic-patc/)). The target is lightweight ActivityGen-style demand in the spirit of SUMO's demand tools ([SUMO ActivityGen](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Demand/Activity-based_Demand_Generation.html)): trips from houses to shops, work and the town edge, scaled by a 24-slot hourly profile on the existing day/night clock. Routing is A* over the junction graph with cost `length/speed × (1 + α·congestion)` plus junction penalties, and closures cost infinity. Only how `next` is chosen changes; car-following stays. "Wander" survives as the Sleepy-village preset.

Configuration is a cascade of sparse layers: built-in defaults (today's constants) → preset → town globals → area → road/junction overrides → time-boxed events. It is resolved into flat `ResolvedTraffic` arrays only when config changes, so the hot loop stays as cheap as the constants it replaces. RoadSense's auto-assigned control is the "Auto" value at the bottom of this cascade, which is why the management note's `JunctionControl.Auto` should exist only in the override type. The model enum should be one shared list: `None, DeadEnd, Gate, Priority, Stop, AllWayStop, RightBeforeLeft, Signal, Roundabout`. Today the three notes each define a different enum.

Globals reuse the `ParamSpec {key,label,group,min,max,step}` pattern in `Model/Params.cs` as a new "Traffic" group (density, max cars, cruise multiplier, patience). The existing marathon closure (`Explore/Course.cs`) becomes the first server-scheduled event type.

The biggest data risk is the one CS2's "Traffic" mod documents. Custom priorities and lane connections are removed "when the network composition has changed" ([krzychu124/Traffic](https://github.com/krzychu124/Traffic)). Porchlight's road identity is a list index in `RoadStroke`. Overrides should therefore be keyed geometrically, *after* RoadSense: `JunctionKey = hash(round(4x), round(4y))`, and `RoadKey = hash(min/max junction keys, kind, midpoint)`. Store them with the `GenParams` hash and generator version they were authored against. Fuzzy re-matching (nearest same-degree node within 1 tile) recovers small shifts, and anything unmatched is shown as "orphaned" rather than dropped. That scheme is a design proposal; no shipped-game source was found.

Persistence fits the existing server. A `town_traffic(town_key, config jsonb, version, updated_at)` table sits beside `towns` in `server/src/multiplayer/tables.ts`. Owner-only `traffic:patch {baseVersion, ops}` messages in `CityRoom.ts` are validated against the `ParamSpec` bounds, and `traffic:config` is broadcast with the full blob on join. The host applies config at the next tick. Because config lives on the server and fixed-time lights derive from the clock, host migration only needs to rebuild `ResolvedTraffic`, clear crossing reservations, and send walkers back to Approach.

The UI should be a "Traffic" mode in `Game/UI/Blueprint.cs`, with the overlay hook points still to be confirmed in code. It has two overlays: volume, and congestion as actual speed over free speed. A Cities: Skylines mod discussion shows these differ and that volume colouring misleads ([Steam Workshop comments](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/comments/1651036644)). Both should use a colour-blind-safe ramp rather than red-to-green ([Amplitude heatmap guide](https://www.amplitude.com/explore/analytics/heatmaps.md)). Junction-control icons work like CS1's Junctions tab ([Paradox Mass Transit diary](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/cities-skylines-mass-transit-dev-diary-1-more-than-just-new-public-transport-types.1005446/)). The tools are inspect, close or one-way, a speed brush, a junction-control cycle with "Reset to auto", a phase editor with preview, and an event planner. Pedestrian toggles per junction (crossing allowed, leading interval) belong in the junction card. Validation warnings ("all exits closed", "road unreachable", "3 orphaned overrides") guard against the deadlock-by-conflicting-tools failure TM:PE users hit ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/255710/discussions/0/5234891274097709772/?ctp=2)). A host-computed 1 Hz scorecard covers flow %, mean delay, stuck cars and pedestrian wait. Lane arrows and connectors are pointless on Porchlight's one-lane-each-way roads.

Determinism rules apply throughout. Iterate cars, walkers and crossings by index. Never let `Dictionary`/`HashSet` order drive decisions; `RoadGraphOps.Incidence` returns a Dictionary, so sort its keys. Use `DetMath` for angles. Break ties by `(arrivalTick, carIndex)`. Draw every stochastic choice once per encounter from the seeded `Rng`. Fix the per-tick order as: signals → walkers commit → crossing occupancy → cars → walkers move. All of this runs only on the host, inside the existing step. Cost is roughly O(cars × nearby crossings + walkers × cars on one road), small next to the current O(n²) `GapAhead`, which per-road sorted lists would also fix.

## Conclusion

The most useful thing the scale fact reveals is that Porchlight is already half-realistic: its cars move at true town speed, and only its pedestrians, its braking and its tailgating gaps are stylised. Real traffic engineering for vehicles (yellow ≈ 2.6–3 s, 2.5-tile junction joins, 1 s headways, SUMO's junction logic) can be imported almost wholesale. Pedestrian behaviour has to be re-derived in game seconds. Many of the notes' "scaled to game time" guesses turn out to be either directly sourced or unnecessary. Webster's formula at near-zero flow justifies short light cycles without any compression argument.

The architectural point is ordering. Control assignment, stable override keys and management UI all depend on a final, clean topology. So RoadSense is not polish. It is the foundation the junction model, the persistence keys and the "Reset to auto" button all stand on, and it should land, behind a generator version, before any of them. The thresholds that remain unsourced (signal score 45, the class quintiles, yield probabilities, impatience timers, IDM accelerations) should be tuned with the seeded defect histograms and replay tests the deterministic architecture makes cheap. The blocked primary sources (MUTCD Part 4 tables, FHWA Signal Timing Manual, sumo.dlr.de pages) are worth a second pass before shipping values presented to players as "realistic".
