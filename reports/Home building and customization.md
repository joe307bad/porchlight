# Layer player homes on the seeded town

Porchlight should build home customization as **a thin, persisted override layer on top of the seeded generator, never a replacement for it**. Each lived-in house gets a small exterior "look" (kit colour variation, then model choice and yard features) and a validated furniture list for each of its ground-floor rooms. Both are keyed by the home's anchor tile, saved over HTTP to a new Postgres table, and announced to the town room with a tiny broadcast so visitors fetch them again. On the design side, the genre has settled on a set of conventions: **snap by default with an opt-in fine/free mode**, a cutaway overhead decorating camera, items that stack on surfaces, unlimited owned-item storage with a capped *placement* budget, and decor earned through ordinary play rather than bought. World of Warcraft's 2025–26 housing is the current reference for all of these. The codebase is unusually well prepared. Interiors are already pure, seeded `RoomPlan`s with overlap and door-clearance rules. Unlocks already flow from ink missions to an `account_unlocks` table. The C# schema decoder needs no codegen. The hard parts are specific and fixable: keeping the `::iso` and `::rooms` RNG streams untouched, getting customized shells out of the static batch, saved layouts that break when a house model changes, and the fact that today **every home row is deleted on move-out and whenever the starter town regenerates**. The recommended plan has four phases: exterior colour, then persistence and sync, then interior placement, then unlock-gated sets and visiting. A first visible slice ships without new art or a schema overhaul.

## Successful games snap first and free second

The strongest current design is World of Warcraft's Midnight housing (early access December 2025, launched March 2, 2026). It has two modes. **Basic** has an optional visible grid that can shrink for tabletop work, snapping by surface type (windows to walls, rugs to floors), and **15° rotation steps "so objects don't accidentally unalign"** ([Warcraft Tavern](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/wow/news/decorating-preview-for-player-housing-in-world-of-warcraft-midnight/); [Icy Veins](https://icy-veins.com/wow/news/midnights-player-housing-a-deep-dive-into-basic-mode)). **Advanced** turns off decor collision and adds free move, rotate and scale gizmos ([Icy Veins interior guide](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/player-housing-interior-guide)). The same split shows up across the genre: The Sims 4 rotates in 45° steps and needs the `bb.moveobjects` cheat to overlap items ([EA Help](https://help.ea.com/de/help/the-sims/the-sims-4/how-to-rotate-sims-4/)), and Elder Scrolls Online added a "Precision Editing" mode on top of its cursor-and-rings editor ([UESP](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Online:Housing_Editor)). Maximal precision has a cost. One comparison found a two-storey house took **about 40 minutes in The Sims 4 and about 90 in inZOI** ([Mein-MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/fans-of-the-sims-are-trying-to-have-fun-at-inzoi-but-are-hitting-a-wall,1246391)). At the other extreme, Townscaper and Tiny Glade remove low-level decisions so that anything a player makes looks good. PC Gamer still found Tiny Glade "can often feel restrictive rather than meditative", and Steam users ask it for an optional grid ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/city-builder/tiny-glade-review)).

Players notice missing features as much as fiddly ones. When Hello Kitty Island Adventure finally let players put items on tables, a reviewer called it **"lifechanging"** ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/life-sim/hello-kitty-island-adventure-is-getting-its-first-major-content-update-today-including-a-new-area-more-visitors-and-the-ability-to-finally-place-items-on-tables)). Disney Dreamlight Valley players complain that "every item has a grid of its own", and that landscaping silently disappears under furniture ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/disney-dreamlight-valley-furniture-problem/)). In FFXIV, partitions are not surfaces and clicking a stack selects the wrong item ([Square Enix forum](https://forum.square-enix.com/ffxiv/threads/443461-Housing-decoration-system-needs-adjustments)). LEGO Fortnite shipped with no move mode and a build menu "two layers deep" that is hard to leave ([Prima Games](https://primagames.com/?p=324542)). Taken together, these define the minimum standard for Porchlight: one consistent grid, typed layers (floor, rug, surface-top, and later wall), items that are never deleted without warning, and a decorate mode one level deep with one obvious exit.

Interiors work best as fixed rooms, not walls the player draws. WoW unlocks room options as house level rises and ships importable starter "Layouts" ([Icy Veins](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/player-housing-guide)). Palia snaps pre-built room modules onto an 11×11 m hub ([HighGround Gaming](https://www.highgroundgaming.com/palia-housing-guide/)). Dreamlight Valley branches rooms off a central room through quest-gated upgrades ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/guides/disney-dreamlight-valley-how-to-upgrade-your-house)). This fits Porchlight well, because its houses are fixed footprints on generated lots. Exteriors stay more constrained than interiors everywhere, because everyone nearby renders them. WoW launched with exterior options described as "fairly limited", disabled exterior lighting for client performance ([Icy Veins](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/player-housing-guide)), and kept a separate, smaller exterior budget, **raised from 200 to 250** after complaints ([Blizzard Watch](https://blizzardwatch.com/2025/12/09/decorators-rejoiced-player-housing-exterior-budget-raised-250)). FFXIV players describe outdoor caps as a rendering problem, because the client draws every item in the ward ([Square Enix forum](https://forum.square-enix.com/ffxiv/threads/141090-Housing-Feedback)).

### Gamepad decorating needs a dedicated camera and a cell cursor

Animal Crossing remains the reference for decorating with a controller. Holding Down enters decorating mode, the camera switches to a near-isometric overhead view, A grabs and drags, and A rotates ([Game8](https://game8.co/games/Animal-Crossing-New-Horizons/archives/285206); [GamePro](https://www.gamepro.de/artikel/animal-crossing-moebel-platzieren-ganz-leicht,3354543.html)). PC-first games add gizmos, rotation rings and mouse-wheel depth ([UESP](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Online:Housing_Editor)). Two complaints recur across games: no multi-select (Animal Crossing stores items one at a time, [iMore](https://www.imore.com/improvements-we-want-see-animal-crossing-new-horizons)) and no copy or reuse (inZOI players ask for material copy and paste, [inZOI forum](https://forum.playinzoi.com/t/topic/9901)). Saved presets and blueprints are now common: WoW Layouts, inZOI presets, the Palia Blueprints tab ([The Games Wiki](https://thegameswiki.com/inzoi/wiki/home-building-guide); [HighGround Gaming](https://www.highgroundgaming.com/palia-housing-guide/)). For a gamepad, a cursor that steps one grid cell at a time works better than a virtual mouse pointer. That last point is engineering judgement; no source in the research documents it.

## Progression works best when decor records how you played

The most effective modern gating attaches decor to progression that already exists. WoW turned twenty years of quests, achievements and raids into decor sources. The Onyxia head trophy, for example, rewards a specific raid achievement ([Warcraft Tavern](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/wow/news/where-to-get-player-housing-decor-in-world-of-warcraft-midnight/)). Players without the expansion could still collect decor before they could claim a home, which worked as a funnel toward buying it ([Escapist](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/news-wow-player-housing-december-2025/)). WoW also adds a progression axis that needs no new items at all: **house level raises the interior and exterior budgets** ([warcraft.wiki.gg](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_C_HousingDecor.GetAllMaxPlacementBudgets)). Palia gates furniture through crafting and plot expansion through quests, and adds "functional furniture" with a gameplay use ([Palia wiki](https://palia.wiki.gg/wiki/Functional_Furniture)). Porchlight's missions (deliveries, chases, firefighting, races) map directly onto this. Each mission family can award a recognizable decor family, such as a fire-helmet coat rack or a race-medal shelf, so a visitor reads a living room as a record of what its owner has done.

The plumbing already exists. `Catalog.UNLOCKS` in C# and `UNLOCKS` in TypeScript are matching whitelists. Ink missions declare `# unlocks: x` and call `unlock("x")`. The server accepts only known, not-yet-held names, persists them in `account_unlocks`, and pushes them to the account's other online characters ([CityRoom.ts:226-234](server/src/multiplayer/CityRoom.ts); [Catalog.cs:145-155](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Runtime/Shared/Explore/Catalog.cs); [server/missions/README.md](server/missions/README.md)). `GarageCars(unlocks)` already gates the fire engine this way. A `FurnitureFor(unlocks)` would follow the same pattern, and the server can check saved layouts against held unlocks exactly as `lookAllowed` checks character looks ([protocol.ts:154-169](server/src/multiplayer/protocol.ts)). Adding a *new* unlock name needs a game build, because the C# list ships with the client. Once a set exists, missions can award it without one, because ink is compiled on the server.

Scoring needs care. Animal Crossing's Happy Home Academy (HHA) scores homes aesthetically: series bonuses of 1,000 points per item once four matching series items are displayed, plus feng shui colour points ([GamerGuides](https://gamerguides.com/animal-crossing-new-leaf/guide/main-street/nooks-homes/happy-home-academy); [Nookipedia](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/F%C3%AAng_shui)). Guides admit feng shui "isn't likely going to make or break your ranking" ([Mic](https://www.mic.com/p/animal-crossing-new-horizons-how-to-improve-your-happy-home-score-22796233)), which makes the system opaque and pushes players toward identical "optimal" rooms. Valheim's Comfort is the better model. It counts *distinct furniture categories* within about 10 m, duplicates do not stack, and each point adds a minute to the Rested buff ([Mobalytics](https://mobalytics.gg/gamebase/guides/valheim-comfort-rested-guide)). That rewards a complete home without dictating its style. A small "cosy" bonus for Porchlight, such as brief sprint stamina after leaving home, counted over seat, bed, light, table and plant categories, gives decorating a purpose without judging taste.

Storage limits buy nothing. Players attack Animal Crossing's storage (5,000 items, later 9,000) and WoW's 7,500-item cap ([Game8](https://game8.co/games/Animal-Crossing-New-Horizons/archives/574484); [Blizzard EU forums](https://eu.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/housing-storage-limit-still-at-7500-ridiculous/602459)). Placement caps are universal and always justified by performance: **ESO 700, doubled for subscribers; Palia 3,500 per plot; WoW a weighted budget where items cost 1, 3 or 5 points** ([ESO forums](https://forums.elderscrollsonline.com/en/discussion/comment/8063588/); [Palia wiki](https://palia.wiki.gg/wiki/Housing_Plot); [CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/decor-budget-filter)). The most common request in every game is to raise the cap. Icy Veins mocked WoW when even Blizzard's own showcase house would not fit under its cap ([Icy Veins](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/news/even-wows-virtual-housing-tour-wont-fit-under-the-new-cap/)). Porchlight already gives every player a house, so it avoids FFXIV's worst problem: plots that sold out within hours and a buggy lottery ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/input/gaming/final-fantasy-14-housing-lottery-technical-issues)). It has no currency today, and should not add one for housing. The model is an **unlock-based collection: a free starter set, unlimited re-placement of anything earned, and a placement budget that grows with completed missions**. If housing is ever monetized, WoW's 2026 shop shows the risks. A $7.50 tree was discounted within days ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/world-of-warcraft/what-the-f-are-these-prices-world-of-warcraft-fans-question-blizzards-usd7-50-trees-prompting-swift-discounts-but-some-say-the-mmos-new-housing-decor-is-still-comically-overpriced)), and selling items Blizzard had promised not to sell caused lasting anger ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/blizzard/blizzard-said-it-wouldnt-sell-real-money-world-of-warcraft-housing-items-tied-to-player-races-it-lied)). Never sell capacity or mission trophies, and write down the "what we won't sell" promise.

## A shared town is already a neighbourhood; it needs discovery and guardrails

Showcasing has moved from friends-only visits to directories people can browse. ESO's Home Tours (August 2024) has Recommended, Browse, Favorites and My Listings tabs. Each player may recommend **10 homes per week** and only from inside the home, and achievements reward touring and recommending ([UESP](https://content1.uesp.net/wiki/Online:Home_Tours)). WoW places houses in persistent public or guild neighbourhoods with monthly collaborative "Endeavors" ([Icy Veins](https://astro.k.icy-veins.com/wow/player-housing-neighborhoods-guide)). Palia layers permissions: Private, Friends or Anyone, plus per-player editor grants. Visitors can sit on furniture but not move it, and cannot visit at all while the owner is offline ([Palia support](https://support.palia.com/hc/en-us/articles/7474242903444-Customization); [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/how-to-visit-other-plots-in-palia/)). ESO's developers note that guest count and furnishing count trade off against performance ([ESO forums](https://forums.elderscrollsonline.com/en/discussion/comment/4069761/)).

Porchlight's town is already a WoW-style neighbourhood. Neighbours' lived-in houses are painted in their own colour on the map, refreshed each time it opens ([PorchlightApp.cs:1124-1168](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Runtime/Game/App/PorchlightApp.cs)). Doors are open to everyone, and private towns already require `mayEnter` ([CityRoom.ts:294-307](server/src/multiplayer/CityRoom.ts)). Because decor would load over HTTP rather than from a live owner session, **visiting an offline owner's home works without extra effort**, an improvement on Palia. The additions that pay off are a per-home "open house" flag, an ESO-style recommend capped at a few per week per player, a "featured homes" highlight on the big map, and a visiting mission such as "drop in on three neighbours" alongside deliveries. Visitors never get edit rights. Since a home belongs to one account and its two residents are that account's characters ([persistence.ts:103-107, 565-585](server/src/multiplayer/persistence.ts)), co-decorating between accounts is a later feature, not a launch concern.

Moderation risk is real even without free text. French press covered "suggestive" decor arrangements within weeks of WoW housing's launch, built from a fixed catalog ([JudgeHype](https://worldofwarcraft.judgehype.com/news/housing-creativite-elfes-de-la-nuit-et-decorations-heu-suggestives-183147/)). Nintendo removes Dream islands for hacked items and for URLs or social handles in comments, sometimes years late ([Animal Crossing World](https://animalcrossingworld.com/2020/07/4-things-to-know-before-uploading-animal-crossing-dreams-advertising-cheating-bulletin-board-ladders/); [Nintendo Life](https://nintendolife.com/news/2026/01/random-an-adults-only-island-has-been-deleted-from-animal-crossing-new-horizons)). For a small team that means: **no free-text signs or house names in the first three phases**; a report-this-home button feeding a review queue; a local "hide this home" that swaps it back to its generated look; delisting from featured homes on reports before any ban; and exteriors kept to curated options, since every passer-by sees them, while interiors are opt-in.

## The codebase gives clean override points and five traps

### What a house is today, and where overrides go

A house in the model is just `Building { id, lotId, footprint, tiles, kind, floors, facing }` ([Types.cs:138-148](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Runtime/Shared/Model/Types.cs)). Its look is chosen at render time. `WorldParts.AddHouses` picks from the 21 suburban Kenney models, splits the frontage into 1–4 prefab pieces, and picks one colour-map `variation` per house (suburban has four) from the sequential `{seed}::iso` RNG ([WorldParts.cs:58-111](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Runtime/Game/Rendering/WorldParts.cs); [Catalog.cs:235-257](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Runtime/Shared/Explore/Catalog.cs)). Every piece has one ground-floor room whose rectangle, doors and windows come from that prefab's import-time `Ground` data ([ModelLibrary.cs:29-71](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Runtime/Game/Rendering/ModelLibrary.cs)). `Interiors.FurnishRoom` then furnishes it from the separate `::rooms` stream, kept separate "so the rest of the town doesn't move when they change" ([World.cs:138-151](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Runtime/Game/Rendering/World.cs)). It produces `Placement { name, x, y, lift, rot, box, flat }`, enforcing inner-rectangle bounds, clear boxes in front of doors, no overlap between non-flat items, and `on`/`lift` stacking ([Interiors.cs:53-72, 321-552](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Runtime/Shared/Explore/Interiors.cs)). That is most of a placement validator already, written as pure C#.

The overrides follow from this. **The exterior** overrides at the `AddHouses` model and variation pick. **The interior** overrides by replacing a room's plan after `FurnishRoom` in `Furnish`, then rebuilding with `InteriorOf`, which caches per room and is not static-batched ([WorldParts.cs:316-322, 380-428](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Runtime/Game/Rendering/WorldParts.cs)). The cheapest exterior options use existing art: the variation index (already supported by `Props.Place(variation:)`), the existing yard features (shed, garden beds, driveway, fence) placed by per-house hashes in `Layout` ([Layout.cs:255-297](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Runtime/Shared/Explore/Layout.cs)), and later a choice among the 21 models. Custom roofs, doors or windows need new art or procedural meshes from `MeshBuilder`/`Palette`, because Kenney bakes them into each prefab ([Editor/Buildings.cs](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Editor/Buildings.cs)). Tinting through `BaseColor` would multiply the whole colour map, roof included. Nobody has yet checked whether the suburban maps keep walls and roof in separate texture regions.

### The traps, and how to avoid each

**RNG drift** is the first trap. Any override that consumes `::iso` or `::rooms` differently shifts trees, lamps, other houses and other rooms for every player. Overrides must call `rng.Int`/`PickModel`/`FurnishRoom` exactly as now and *then* replace the result. **Static batching** is the second. The whole static town, shells included, goes through `StaticBatchingUtility.Combine` at the end of `Build()` ([World.cs:178-179](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Runtime/Game/Rendering/World.cs)). Swapping a shell later is impossible unless that house was left out of the combined batch. In URP, static batching also takes precedence over and disables the SRP Batcher and instancing for those objects ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/should-you-enable-static-batching-in-an-srp-project/272849)). The fix is to fetch looks before `Build()`, and to build lived-in, customized homes under `root` rather than `still`. With at most 100 clients per town, that is a bounded number of unbatched shells drawn through the SRP Batcher. **Model-dependent rooms** are the third. A saved layout is only valid for the model, and split, that produced its room. So each layout must carry a shell signature (model, split, piece index). On a mismatch, its items return to the collection with a notice, never a silent delete (the Dreamlight Valley lesson). **Home rows are disposable** is the fourth. `emptyHouse` deletes a home when nobody lives there, and `settleStarterTown` deletes and randomly reassigns every home whenever the starter town regenerates ([persistence.ts:347, 521-563](server/src/multiplayer/persistence.ts)). Any generator change trips the `homes` check and forces `npm run town` ([Program.cs:176-197](unity/Tools~/Checks/Program.cs)). So customization must live with the *account*, not inside the `homes` row. **Identity** is the fifth. Building ids are generation order and get renumbered, so homes are already named by anchor tile ([Homes.cs:1-30](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Runtime/Shared/Explore/Homes.cs)). Customization must use the same key.

### Data model, transport and validation

The research supports a sparse list of `{catalogId, variant, quantized transform, optional parent/anchor}` in a versioned envelope, the pattern behind Minecraft's `DataVersion` migrations ([Lodestone chunk format](https://team-lodestone.github.io/Documentation/Java/Anvil_Docs/1.16+/Chunk_Format)) and Valheim blueprint mods that rebuild from prefab ID plus transform ([PlanBuild](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/MathiasDecrock/PlanBuild/v/0.14.5)). Anchoring items to semantic handles (room, wall edge) rather than world coordinates lets them survive regeneration. For Porchlight, that means a pure `HomeInterior` in `Shared/Explore` holding `SavedItem { name, u, v, rot, on }`. The coordinates are in the existing room frame (u along the front, v into the room), quantized to 1/16 tile, about 25 cm at 1 tile ≈ 4 m. It provides `Validate` and `ToPlan` by factoring `FurnishRoom`'s private `Put` into a shared helper. **Stable string names** (`"furniture/sofa"`) go in the database, even though the catalog's wire indices are append-only ([Catalog.cs:56-59](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Runtime/Shared/Explore/Catalog.cs)), because names survive catalog reshuffles. Eighty items at about 40 bytes each come to about 3 KB of JSON. That is over the current **1 KB HTTP body cap** ([accounts.ts:64-77](server/src/multiplayer/accounts.ts)), so the save route needs its own limit of about 16 KB. Saves stay well under Postgres's roughly 2 KB TOAST threshold for exterior looks, and are debounced for interiors, so whole-value JSONB rewrites ([pganalyze](https://pganalyze.com/blog/5mins-postgres-jsonb-toast)) cost little at one write per save rather than per edit.

The technical research recommends a server-authoritative Colyseus room per house, holding a `MapSchema` of decor and applying intent ops. That is the right shape for live co-op decorating. **Porchlight should not start there.** Its server holds no town geometry, since only the C# generator can produce rooms, and those rooms depend on prefab `Ground` data that lives in the gitignored Unity library. Movement is not wall-authoritative either ([movement.ts:24, 67-83](server/src/multiplayer/movement.ts)). The server can therefore validate *structure*: ownership (`homes.playerId === account`), item count, known names, held unlocks and payload size. Every client runs the same `HomeInterior.Validate` and drops anything spatially invalid. A cheater could at worst save a layout with overlapping furniture, which other clients' validation removes before display. Transport follows the existing `RefreshNeighbours` precedent: extend `GET /mp/towns/:id/homes` with each home's small look, fetch furniture lazily within `INTERIOR_REACH`, save with `PUT`, and have the room broadcast `MSG_HOME {x, y, rev}` so visitors fetch again. That is far below the **4 KB WebSocket cap**, and keeps decor out of the 33 ms schema patch ([index.ts:50-52](server/src/multiplayer/index.ts); [CityRoom.ts:1-18](server/src/multiplayer/CityRoom.ts)). This also avoids Colyseus 0.16's StateView, which its own announcement says is not yet optimized for large datasets ([Colyseus blog](https://colyseus.io/blog/colyseus-016-is-here/)). If co-op editing arrives later, Figma's model of last-writer-wins per property, in server order, is enough. CRDTs are not needed ([Evan Wallace](https://madebyevan.com/figma/how-figmas-multiplayer-technology-works/)).

On rendering, every interior is built at load, 64 per step ([World.cs:154-160](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Runtime/Game/Rendering/World.cs)), so custom furniture must load lazily. Kenney furniture shares colour-map materials, which suits the SRP Batcher. GPU instancing on WebGL requires WebGL 2 ([Unity manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/es/2017.1/Manual/GPUInstancing.html)). The GPU Resident Drawer needs compute shaders, so the WebGL build cannot use it ([URP docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.universal@17.0/manual/gpu-resident-drawer.html)). Catalog thumbnails can reuse the existing `Portraits` render-texture tiles behind the overlay `Choose` grid.

### Controls that fit the existing pad map

Decorate mode should be one more modal branch in `ExploreView.ReadPad`, next to the map and chooser branches, available only when the player stands in a room of `HouseOfHome()` ([ExploreView.cs:1720-1800](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Runtime/Game/App/ExploreView.cs)). Entering a room already hides the shell, and a room camera already exists, so the cutaway view comes nearly free. The table maps actions onto the existing bindings so habits carry over.

| Action | Gamepad | Mouse / keyboard |
|---|---|---|
| Enter / leave decorate mode | Hold d-pad down in own home; B leaves | A new whitelisted key in `GameInput.Keys`; Esc leaves |
| Move cursor | Left stick, one cell per `Flick`, repeat on hold | Raycast to floor, snap to 1/16 tile |
| Pick up / place | A | Left click |
| Rotate 90° (fine 15° while LT held) | LB / RB | Q / E or wheel; Shift for fine |
| Catalog | Y opens the `Choose` grid (LB/RB page, A picks) | Tab / click toolbar |
| Store to collection | X | Delete |
| Cycle stacked items under cursor | D-pad up | Alt-click |
| Rotate / zoom camera | D-pad left/right, up/down (as now) | Right drag / wheel with Ctrl |
| Undo / redo | View (hold for redo) | Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y |
| Save and exit | Menu | Enter |

One conflict needs a decision. The Firefox Xbox Bluetooth workaround treats a 0.4 s hold of Y as LB ([GameInput.cs:15-243](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Runtime/Game/App/GameInput.cs)). If Y opens the catalog, test that path on Firefox, or move the catalog to a press-and-release action. The undo stack lives on the client only and records commands; persistent safety comes from the server keeping a few prior revisions.

## A four-phase plan that ships colour first

| Phase | Scope | Main files | Exit test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1: Exterior look** | Pure `HomeLook { variation, model = -1, shed, garden, fence }` with validation; `AddHouses` override after the RNG calls; lived-in custom homes built outside `still`; "Your house" card using the `Choose` grid | `Shared/Explore/HomeLook.cs`, `WorldParts.cs`, `World.cs`, `FrontDoor`/`ExploreOverlay` | `towns 60` unchanged with overrides applied; other houses' picks are byte-identical |
| **P2: Persistence and sync** | `home_decor` table keyed by **(playerId, townId)**, holding anchor x,y, look jsonb, rooms jsonb (each with a shell signature), rev and updatedAt, plus the last N revisions; routes `GET` (with homes) and `PUT .../home/look` with a 16 KB limit; `MSG_HOME` broadcast via a `GameDeps` hook; `MemoryPersistence` parity | `tables.ts` + migration `0005`, `persistence.ts`, `accounts.ts`, `protocol.ts`/`Protocol.cs`, `Account.cs`, `Multiplayer.cs` | Look survives move-out and starter-town regeneration (re-applied to the new house); visitor sees change within one fetch |
| **P3: Interior placement** | `HomeInterior` validate/encode, shared `Put` helper, decorate mode, `RebuildInterior` that patches `Solids`, per-room budget meter, return-to-collection on signature mismatch, starter layout = the procedural plan | `Interiors.cs`, `HomeInterior.cs`, `WorldParts.cs`, `ExploreView.cs`, `GameInput.cs`, new `Checks` subcommand | Round-trip and validation over synthetic rooms in CI; 80-item room holds frame time on WebGL |
| **P4: Earned sets and visiting** | `furniture-<set>` unlocks plus `FurnitureFor(unlocks)`, mission trophies, budget grows with `mission_completions`, cosy category bonus, open-house flag, rate-limited recommends, featured homes, report/hide | `Catalog.cs`, `protocol.ts`, ink missions, overlay map, new report route | Server rejects saves with unheld items; reports delist a home |

Keying `home_decor` by account and town, not by the `homes` row, is the key decision. Exterior looks carry over unchanged when a household is rehoused. Room layouts re-apply when the new shell signature matches. When it does not, items return to the unlimited collection. Nothing a player earned is ever lost to a regeneration. Wall-mounted items (excluded from the library today, [Catalog.cs:1-7](unity/Assets/Porchlight/Runtime/Shared/Explore/Catalog.cs)), upper floors and new room modules come after P4, and only if the ground-floor version shows demand.

The risks to watch are concrete. RNG drift is guarded by a check that renders a town with and without overrides and compares every non-overridden pick. The model-swap change in P1 alters collision and door positions on every client that renders the override, so it ships behind a flag after colour. The `Checks` tool cannot validate against real rooms, because `Ground` data lives only in the Unity library. A small exported dump of room rectangles, or synthetic rooms, closes that gap. Shared code must stay engine-free C# 9 ([Checks.csproj](unity/Tools~/Checks/Checks.csproj)). The 1 KB and 4 KB payload caps must be raised only on the new route. Multi-piece houses mean one home has up to four rooms. Kenney's roughly 80 furniture models and four suburban colourways put a ceiling on variety, and only new art or `MeshBuilder` pieces raise it. Several of the cited budget and storage figures for WoW changed across patches, so treat them as direction rather than targets.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "what editor should Porchlight build?" to "what is a player's home, given that the generator owns the house?" The answer is that a home is an account-owned, versioned set of overrides that the generator's output is filtered through. That framing removes the scariest problems. Regeneration, rehousing and catalog growth become migration steps, not data loss. The server never needs to understand geometry, because every client runs the same pure validator. The genre's hard-won lessons (snap first, unlimited storage, generous budgets, decor as a trophy of play) also turn out to be the cheapest options to build on Porchlight's existing unlock and interior code.

The open bet is how far Porchlight goes beyond a curated shell. Townscaper and Tiny Glade show that procedural polish can make any player choice look intentional, and a later phase that drives `MeshBuilder` trim, porches and roofs from player toggles would suit a low-poly seeded town better than imported prefabs. That phase should wait until P3 shows how many players actually decorate and how many simply want their house to look like theirs from the street.
