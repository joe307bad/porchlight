## Codebase baseline (read from source, for mapping)

- `Traffic.cs`: `CRUISE = 2.4` tiles/s, `GAP = 1.6` tiles, MAX_CARS 70, `PATIENCE = 4` s before a U-turn, `LANE_HALF = 0.42`. Target speed is piecewise-linear in `min(GapAhead, BlockedAhead)`: 0 below GAP, a ramp up to 2·GAP, then CRUISE. Speed then relaxes toward the target with `speed += (target - speed)·min(1, 3dt)`, a first-order lag with no explicit acceleration or braking bound. `BlockedAhead` takes point obstacles lying within `along ∈ (0, 4.0]` tiles and `|lateral| < 0.42`, measured along the car's *current heading* (not its future path), so it misses anything on the far side of a corner curve. `GapAhead` looks only at cars on the same road and direction, so there is no look-ahead across a junction.
- `Actors.cs` `Pedestrian`: pace `rng.Range(0.9, 1.5)` tiles/s. Each tick, with probability `dt·0.02` (about one crossing every 50 s per walker), `walk.CrossStreet()` crosses straight over mid-block with no check for traffic. Junction crossings come from `AtEnd()` via `net.links`/`net.crossings` (`JUNCTION_CROSSING_COST = 2`, mid-block `CROSSING_COST = 8`).
- `ExploreView.cs` ~L1902–1907: the obstacles are other traffic cars (driven by the player and others), the local player when on foot, other players, `Obstacles` and parked cars. **Townsfolk are not included**, so cars drive through crossing NPCs.
- Scale: a car at 2.4 tiles/s is only about 2× a walker's speed (0.9–1.5). In real life the ratio is about 10× (13.4 m/s at 30 mph against about 1.2 m/s). Any real-world gap or time threshold therefore has to be translated as **time-to-arrival in sim seconds**, not as distance. Crossing a 1-tile street takes about 0.7–1.1 s and a 2-tile main road about 1.3–2.2 s at walker pace.

## Car-following and stopping models suitable for games (IDM, Gipps, Krauss)

### Takeaway
Replace the linear ramp with the Intelligent Driver Model (IDM). Treat every "reason to stop" as a virtual stationary leader: a stop line, a pedestrian in the conflict zone, a reserved crossing. One collision-free formula then gives smooth approach, stopping and pull-away for all cases. SUMO's default Krauss model is the alternative: it is a hard-safe "safe speed plus random dawdle" scheme, also cheap and deterministic once dawdling is driven by the seeded Rng.

### Cited Findings
- The IDM is "a time-continuous car-following traffic flow model for the simulation of freeway and urban traffic". Each parameter maps to a regime: desired speed v0 to free flow, time gap T to steady following, minimum gap s0 to creeping and standing traffic, a and b to non-steady flow. The minimum distance s0 "is significant for low velocities only", and the "IDM brakes stronger than b if the gap becomes too small. This braking strategy makes the IDM collision-free" — [Wikipedia: Intelligent driver model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_driver_model) (via search snippet); [Kesting/Treiber/Helbing, Enhanced IDM (arXiv 0912.3613)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0912.3613); [Twenty-Five Years of the IDM (arXiv 2506.05909)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.05909)
- **IDM equations** (standard published form, from prior knowledge; the pages above were not fetchable):
  - `a_IDM = a · [1 − (v/v0)^δ − (s*/s)²]`
  - `s* = s0 + max(0, v·T + v·Δv / (2·√(a·b)))`, where s is the net gap to the leader and Δv = v − v_leader (positive when closing). δ = 4 is typical.
  - For a stationary target such as a stop line or a waiting pedestrian, use v_leader = 0 and s = distance to the stop point.
- Typical published parameter sets: v0 30 m/s, T 1.5 s, s0 2 m, a 0.73 m/s², b 1.67 m/s², δ 4 (Treiber reference set); T 1.6 s, s0 2 m, a 0.7, b 1.7 (lane-change study); T 1.5 s (car) / 2.0 s (truck), b 2.0 m/s² (ACC comparison). No city-specific calibration turned up in the search — [search summary of IDM sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_driver_model), [arXiv 0912.3613](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0912.3613)
- SUMO's default car model is Krauss. Defaults: accel 2.6 m/s², decel 4.5 m/s², emergencyDecel 9 m/s², sigma 0.5 ("driver imperfection, 0 denotes perfect driving"), tau 1.0 s ("desired (minimum) time headway … based on the net space between leader back and follower front"), minGap 2.5 m, passenger length 5 m — [SUMO docs: Vehicle types](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Definition_of_Vehicles%2C_Vehicle_Types%2C_and_Routes.md); [SUMO Vehicle Type Parameter Defaults](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Vehicle_Type_Parameter_Defaults.md)
- "The Krauss model selects a speed that ensures minGap can always be maintained". For Krauss, decel "is a hard limit whereas for other models such as IDM the decel bound is less strict". The safe speed accounts for the leader's braking via `apparentDecel`. If braking beyond decel is needed, the vehicle may brake up to `emergencyDecel`. Setting tau below the reaction time "leads to unsafe driving … high deceleration or even collisions" — [SUMO docs: Car-Following Models](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Car-Following-Models/index.md)
- The SUMO Krauss code has a `vsafe` function plus a dawdle step that subtracts `rand · sigma · accel` (floored at 0) — [SUMO doxygen MSCFModel_Krauss](https://sumo.dlr.de/doxygen/de/d67/_m_s_c_f_model___krauss_8cpp_source.html) (via search snippet)
- **Krauss safe speed** (standard form, from prior knowledge): `v_safe = v_l + (g − v_l·τ) / ((v + v_l)/(2b) + τ)`, then `v_des = min(v + a·dt, v_safe, v_max)`, then `v = max(0, v_des − σ·a·dt·rand)`.
- SUMO stops vehicles `jmStoplineGap = 1 m` before a priority or traffic-light stop line, and `jmStoplineGapMinor = 0 m` at a non-priority one. `jmStopSignWait` is the minimum standstill at a stop sign — [SUMO junction model parameters](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Definition_of_Vehicles%2C_Vehicle_Types%2C_and_Routes.md)
- Gipps: no source text could be retrieved in this session (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- **Mapping to tiles.** If a tile is about 4 m (one lane, since streets are 1 tile wide), CRUISE 2.4 tiles/s is about 9.6 m/s if left unscaled. Suggested starting game parameters in tiles and seconds: v0 = 2.4, T = 0.6–0.8 s (the game is time-compressed, so 1.5 s would leave huge gaps), s0 = 0.5 tile bumper-to-bumper (car length is about 1 tile, so centre-to-centre is about 1.5, close to today's GAP of 1.6), a = 2.0 tiles/s², b = 3.0 tiles/s², δ = 4, plus a hard clamp at an emergency decel of about 8 tiles/s². Tune by eye.
- **Integrate with the "ballistic" update** (standard practice in Treiber's simulator; from prior knowledge): `s += max(0, v·dt + ½·a·dt²)`, `v = max(0, v + a·dt)`. This keeps speeds non-negative at 50–60 Hz fixed steps. Clamp a to at least −b_max, and handle s ≤ 0 by setting v = 0.
- **Stop line or pedestrian as a virtual leader.** Compute `s = dist_to_stop_point − s0_ped`. `stop_point` is the near edge of the crosswalk, or the projected point on the lane where the pedestrian's path crosses the car's path, minus a 0.3–0.5 tile buffer. Feed `min(s_leader, s_virtual)` into IDM. This replaces both the GAP ramp and the `PATIENCE` U-turn logic for pedestrian cases, since a pedestrian will clear.
- Krauss suits Porchlight if hard stopping distances are a priority. Its "safe" guarantee is that a car never needs more than decel to avoid the leader. IDM looks smoother and is easier to tune. Either needs only `double` maths plus seeded noise, so both are deterministic.
- Add per-car variety deterministically. Sample v0 × N(1, 0.1), the equivalent of SUMO speedDev 0.1, and T × U(0.8, 1.2) from the seeded Rng at spawn.

## Pedestrian crossing behaviour (gap acceptance, waiting, crosswalk vs mid-block, speeds, looking, groups)

### Takeaway
Pedestrians should walk to the kerb, stop and look (about 0.5–1.5 s), then accept a gap only if the nearest car's time-to-arrival exceeds `crossing_time + safety margin`. Field critical gaps are about 3.6–4.3 s on arterials, against an HCM-style `L/Sp + ts` (ts ≈ 3 s) that is known to be conservative. Impatience should shrink the margin over time. Walking speeds of about 1.2 m/s with 3.0–3.5 ft/s (0.9–1.1 m/s) for the slow 15th percentile give realistic spread, matching Porchlight's 0.9–1.5 pace range.

### Cited Findings
- Walking speed: the 2009 MUTCD design speed is 3.5 ft/s (1.07 m/s), down from 4.0. TCRP/NCHRP recommends 3.5 ft/s for the general population and 3.0 ft/s (0.9 m/s) for older or less able people. 15th percentile speeds are 3.03 ft/s for over-60s and 3.77 ft/s for younger people. 2.9 ft/s is recommended where nearly all pedestrians are over 65 — [NAP / TCRP-NCHRP report](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/13962/chapter/10); [AAA Foundation: Pedestrian Signal Safety for Older Persons](https://aaafoundation.org/pedestrian-signal-safety-older-persons/); [Noyce et al. 2006](https://files.topslab.wisc.edu/publications/2006/noyce_2006_1826.pdf)
- An FHWA study recommends 1.2 m/s (3.9 ft/s) for most conditions and 1.0 m/s where many pedestrians are over 65, noting that the HCM then used 1.4 m/s — [FHWA-RD-98-107](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/pedbike/98107/section2.cfm)
- SUMO defaults: pedestrian desiredMaxSpeed 1.39 m/s (5 km/h), speedDev 0.1, accel 1.5 m/s², decel 2 m/s², minGap 0.25 m, width 0.478 m — [SUMO Vehicle Type Parameter Defaults](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Vehicle_Type_Parameter_Defaults.md)
- An HCM 2010 uncontrolled-crossing worksheet uses pedestrian start-up plus end clearance time `ts = 3 s` alongside crosswalk length and walking speed. The critical headway structure is crossing time plus a safety margin — [HCM worksheet (Ramsey Co.)](https://public.destinyhosted.com/ramsedocs/2017/PW/20170620_8333/7611_Copy%20of%204501_LOS%20worksheet%20for%20Armstrong%20and%20161st%20crosswalk2.pdf); [search summary](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3141/2002-16). The exact HCM equation text was not retrieved. The commonly cited form `t_c = L/S_p + t_s` is from prior knowledge.
- On high-speed arterials (Safety Science 2016) the temporal critical gap is 3.6–4.3 s and the spatial gap 60–73 m, depending on method. Both are "much lower than … HCM 2010" values, and deterministic methods (Raff's) give smaller values than probabilistic ones (logit/ML) — [IITH: critical gap estimation at uncontrolled mid-block crossings](https://publications.iith.ac.in/publication/critical-gap-estimation-for-pedestrians-at-uncontrolled-mid-block-1) (via search snippet)
- Gap acceptance depends on gap size, waiting time and "caution". Critical gaps are larger at unsignalised intersections than mid-block (Tehran, mixed logit) — [arXiv 1905.09403](https://export.arxiv.org/abs/1905.09403). Pedestrians accept smaller gaps in front of motorcycles, and vehicle type, yielding behaviour, waiting time, age and speeds all matter (Bangkok 2025) — [KMUTT](https://kirim.kmutt.ac.th/converis/portal/detail/Publication/1508997451?lang=en_GB). Binary logit models outperform a fixed critical-gap model — [TRID 758915](https://trid.trb.org/view.aspx?id=758915)
- In an FHWA study of more than 70,000 crossings, 8.93% involved a vehicle yielding to a pedestrian and 0.98% involved a pedestrian yielding to a vehicle. More than half of pedestrian fatalities happen at unmarked locations away from intersections — [FHWA-HRT-13-098](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/13098/001.cfm) (via search snippet)
- SUMO pedestrians at an *unprioritised* crossing cross only "if the whole length of the crossing is free of vehicles for the whole time needed to cross" (described as conservative). At priority (zebra) crossings they "cross without regard for vehicles". Impatience applies to pedestrians too: `impatience = clamp(base + waitingTime/120 s)`, and "impatient pedestrians may cross the street even though it forces cars to brake" — [SUMO Pedestrians](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Simulation/Pedestrians.md); [SUMO vehicle types: impatience](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Definition_of_Vehicles%2C_Vehicle_Types%2C_and_Routes.md)
- SUMO striping pedestrians "wait in front of crossings in a wide queue" and walk side by side. Dawdling defaults to 0.2 of max speed — [SUMO Pedestrians](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Simulation/Pedestrians.md)

### Inferences
- **Proposed walker crossing state machine** (deterministic, per walker): `Approach` (walk to the kerb point) → `Look` (stand 0.4–1.2 s from Rng, turn the head or body left then right; a cheap, very readable tell) → `Wait` (each tick, test the gap) → `Cross` (commit, maybe +15% speed) → `Done`. Rarely, `Abort/Hurry` if a car appears mid-crossing: speed up and never turn back, to keep it simple.
- **Gap test in game time.** For each car approaching the crossing point within the look-ahead, compute `tta = dist_along_lane / max(speed, 0.3)`. Accept if `min tta > t_cross + margin`. Here `t_cross = road_width_tiles / pace + 0.3`, and the margin starts around 1.5 s and shrinks linearly to about 0.5 s over 10–20 s of waiting (impatience, following the SUMO form). Treat cars already yielding (decelerating, or stopped with a flag) as accepted gaps. Optionally use a logistic acceptance `P = 1/(1+exp(−k(tta − tc)))` with seeded rand for variety instead of a hard threshold.
- **Mid-block vs junction.** FHWA's data shows yielding is rarer at unmarked mid-block sites. Make mid-block crossers stricter (larger margin) and keep the 8 vs 2 routing cost so most crossings happen at junctions. Real proportions of mid-block crossing were not found (see Gaps). A mix of roughly 70–85% at junctions is a design choice, not a sourced figure.
- **Groups.** When one walker accepts, any walker waiting at the same kerb within about 1 tile who is heading across should start within 0–0.5 s. This is the "follow the leader" crossing seen in the SUMO queue behaviour and commonly in the real world (an inference, unsourced). It also reduces how often cars have to restart.

## Driver yielding, conflict zones and reservations, deadlock avoidance

### Takeaway
SUMO's approach is cheap and maps directly onto Porchlight. A car must not enter a crossing (conflict zone) while any pedestrian is on it, or is walking toward the conflict point and is within `jmCrossingGap` (default 10 m). Pedestrians only start an unprioritised crossing if it is clear for their whole crossing time. Jams resolve through impatience and a "jammed" timeout (10 s on crossings) after which agents push through. Model yielding probabilistically per car and per crossing type.

### Cited Findings
- SUMO: "Vehicles are prevented from driving across a pedestrian crossing which is occupied by pedestrians. If a pedestrian is found which is not yet past the intersection point … but within a threshold distance to that point (`jmCrossingGap`) the crossing is considered to be blocked." The default `jmCrossingGap` is 10 m: "Minimum distance to pedestrians that are walking towards the conflict point… the lower the value, the braver (more aggressive) the driver." — [SUMO Pedestrians](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Simulation/Pedestrians.md); [SUMO junction model params](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Definition_of_Vehicles%2C_Vehicle_Types%2C_and_Routes.md)
- SUMO network structure: crossings are lanes of type *crossing*. *walkingareas* connect sidewalks and crossings at a junction, and pedestrians route through them. Crossing types: priority (zebra), non-priority, traffic light. `--crossings.guess` adds crossings automatically at junctions, but on roads faster than 50 km/h only at signalised ones. "Cars will interact by pedestrians (by slowing down or stopping) when encountering them on a shared road lane or when passing a walkingarea that is used by pedestrians" — [SUMO Pedestrians](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Simulation/Pedestrians.md)
- SUMO mitigations for deadlock and jams: on crossings and walkingareas pedestrians reserve 1/3 of the space for oncoming pedestrians. A pedestrian stuck for 300 s (10 s on a crossing, 1 s on narrow elements) becomes "jammed" and moves at 1/4 speed regardless of obstacles until free — [SUMO Pedestrians](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Simulation/Pedestrians.md)
- Other SUMO junction knobs: `jmIgnoreFoeProb` / `jmIgnoreFoeSpeed` (ignore a right-of-way foe with probability p if it is slower than v; also applies to pedestrians), `jmIgnoreJunctionFoeProb`, `jmTimegapMinor` (1 s), `jmAdvance` (creep toward the conflict point timed to arrive just as the foe clears), `jmExtraGap`, `impatience` (driver: `clamp(base + wait/180 s)`; at 1 the driver uses any collision-safe gap) — [SUMO junction model params](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Definition_of_Vehicles%2C_Vehicle_Types%2C_and_Routes.md)
- The SUMO developer said the 1 s `jmTimegapMinor` default had no calibration behind it. German guidelines cite about 6.7 s for the first car in a queue to cross ahead of priority traffic and 3.3 s for a following car — [sumo-user list](https://www.eclipse.org/lists/sumo-user/msg03919.html)
- Real yielding rates: about 8.9% of 70k+ crossings involved a vehicle yielding, and yielding was significantly more common at marked intersections than at unmarked mid-block sites — [FHWA-HRT-13-098](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/13098/001.cfm). High-visibility markings raise yielding, yielding falls as speed rises, and above an 85th-percentile speed of 30 mph markings alone had no significant effect — [FHWA 10-043 summary](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/pedbike/10043/003.cfm). With in-street R1-6a signs, baseline yielding averaged 85% nearside and 89% farside (Oregon 2026) — [ROSAP dot/92255](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/92255) (via search snippets)
- Cities: Skylines (player reports, not developer documentation): unsignalised crossings in dense areas can form a "continuous blockade of people crossing". In C:S II pedestrians jaywalking caused traffic jams (a forum thread was reportedly tagged "As Designed"), and a mod exists to stop it — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/cities-skylines-2/fix-pedestrians-mod); [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/articles/newsjp/20231115-272227/); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/255710/discussions/0/3781371514573567813)

### Inferences
- **Crossing reservation for Porchlight.** Give each crossing (junction mouth, plus a mid-block crossing as a transient one) an `occupants` set and a `committed` set:
  1. A walker in `Wait` passes the gap test, then adds itself to `committed` and starts `Cross`.
  2. A car computes the distance `d` to every crossing on its path ahead (current road, plus the next road once `car.next` is chosen). If the crossing is committed or occupied (pedestrian on it, or within 10 m ≈ 2.5 tiles of the car's conflict point and walking toward it), it adds a virtual leader at `d − 0.4` tiles.
  3. **No mid-crossing yield flip-flop.** Once a car is within its braking distance `v²/(2b)` of the crossing and has not yet seen a commitment, it is itself "committed": it is added to the crossing's `carsCommitted` and walkers' gap tests treat it as unyielding. This removes the classic deadlock where both wait or both go.
  - This is the same two-phase "check, then commit" logic SUMO uses (pedestrians need the whole crossing free for the whole time; cars are blocked by occupied crossings).
- **Yield probability.** Per car per encounter, roll `pYield` once (seeded) when the walker is waiting at the kerb and not yet committed: about 0.8–0.9 at a junction mouth (matching the "marked/signed" figures) and about 0.3–0.5 mid-block. Yielding cars slow to stop; the walker sees decel and treats it as a gap. Non-yielders drive through and the walker keeps waiting. Committed walkers are always stopped for (safety plus believability).
- **Deadlock and gridlock guards:** (a) the walker impatience margin floor (as in SUMO); (b) a car waiting more than about 6 s at a crossing gets a short "nudge" window. Walkers who have not yet committed must let it pass (a "priority token" that alternates), mirroring SUMO's jam timeout; (c) never U-turn because of a pedestrian. Exclude pedestrian virtual leaders from `car.wait` so the `PATIENCE` U-turn only triggers for static blockers; (d) don't-block-the-box: cars don't enter a junction curve unless the exit lane has at least one car length free, preventing grid-locked junctions as walkers hold the exit crosswalk.
- **Platooning walkers** (C:S lesson): a stream of walkers can starve a road indefinitely. Cap it: once a crossing has had walkers for more than about 8 s continuously while a car waits, new walkers do not commit until the car passes.

## How shipped games do it (sensing, look-ahead, reactions, cheap tricks)

### Takeaway
Little primary developer material could be retrieved in this session. The common themes found are local raycast or box-sensing avoidance on fixed lanes, plus pedestrians constrained to sidewalks and crosswalks with simple rule obedience. City builders show what goes wrong without throttling (pedestrian blockades). Porchlight should use a cheap forward "sensor box" swept along the future path (not the heading), plus a few reaction animations and sounds.

### Cited Findings

- A Gamasutra article, "AI Madness: Using AI to Bring Open-City Racing to Life" (Midtown Madness-era ambient traffic and pedestrians), exists, but gamedeveloper.com was blocked and its contents were not verified — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/ai-madness-using-ai-to-bring-open-city-racing-to-life)

The TM:PE mod lets players ban crossings per junction — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/255710/discussions/0/3781371514573567813); [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/cities-skylines-2/fix-pedestrians-mod)

### Inferences (design suggestions, not sourced)
- **Path-swept sensor:** sample the car's *future* lane path (via `Lane`/`Corner` at `done + k·0.25` for k up to the look-ahead `v·T + v²/(2b) + s0`). Test obstacles within LANE_HALF of those samples. This fixes `BlockedAhead` missing walkers around corners.
- **Reactions** (cheap, readable): a walker waves the car on, or the driver flashes headlights when yielding; a honk if a car is stopped by a dawdling or committed walker for more than 3 s (seeded chance), with the walker hurrying (pace × 1.3) on hearing it. A near-miss (car within 0.6 tiles of a walker at v > 1) triggers a startle hop: the existing `lift` or jump plus a step back to the kerb.
- **"Eyes" tell:** walkers turn their heading toward oncoming traffic during `Look`/`Wait`, using the same `heading +=` easing as `lookAt` in `Pedestrian.Update`.

## Multiplayer, determinism and performance (~70 cars, dozens of walkers)

### Takeaway
All of the above is O(cars × nearby crossings + walkers × nearby cars) with simple `double` arithmetic. It runs on the host only, inside the existing deterministic step, and clients keep receiving poses at about 10 Hz. Only new transient state (walker crossing phase, crossing reservations) needs to be included in host-migration snapshots.

### Cited Findings
- SUMO's striping model updates each pedestrian in a single pass per direction, front first, and limits the vehicle look-around to 60 m. Stochastic elements (dawdling 0.2, sigma 0.5) come from seeded random draws — [SUMO Pedestrians](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Simulation/Pedestrians.md); [MSPModel_Striping source](https://sumo.dlr.de/doxygen/d8/dc5/_m_s_p_model___striping_8cpp_source.html) (60 m look-around via search snippet)
- SUMO's per-step re-rolled `jmIgnoreFoeProb` shows the pattern of per-step stochastic decisions drawn from the simulation RNG — [SUMO junction model params](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Definition_of_Vehicles%2C_Vehicle_Types%2C_and_Routes.md)

### Inferences
- **Determinism:** draw all random choices (yield roll, look duration, logistic acceptance) from the existing seeded `Rng`, in a fixed iteration order (cars by index, walkers by index, crossings by id). Roll *once per encounter*, store the result (`car.yieldTo = walkerId`, `walker.lookLeft`), and do not re-roll per frame, so behaviour is frame-rate independent. Avoid `Math.Pow` and `Math.Sqrt` divergence by using DetMath where the codebase already does. For IDM, δ = 4 can be computed as `(v/v0)²` squared, and √(ab) is a precomputed constant.
- **Order of update:** walkers decide (commit) → crossings update occupancy → cars update (IDM with virtual leaders) → walkers move. This avoids one-frame races where both commit.
- **Performance:** build a per-road bucket of crossings (static), then each tick a per-road list of committed and occupied crossings. Cars on road r check only that list plus the next road's list: about 70 × 2–4 checks. Walkers waiting at a kerb check cars on that road only (`cars` filtered by `road == r`, or maintain a `byRoad` index each tick): dozens × about 5. This is negligible compared with the existing `GapAhead`, which is O(n²) at about 4,900 pair checks; per-road sorted lists would fix that too.
- **Sync:** clients render walkers and cars from snapshots. Add `walker.phase` (Waiting/Looking/Crossing) so remote clients can play look or wave animations, and a `car.honk` event flag. On host handover (`Traffic.Adopt`), clear reservations and let walkers re-enter `Approach` so state can't be inconsistent.

- No measured cost figures for comparable C# implementations were found.

## Concrete recommended changes for Porchlight

### Takeaway
1. Feed walkers (position + intent) into traffic. 2. Replace the GAP ramp with IDM plus virtual leaders. 3. Add kerb-wait, look and gap-accept to `CrossStreet` and junction crossings. 4. Add a two-phase crossing reservation with car commitment and impatience guards. 5. Sweep the sensor along the future path. Each change is small and local to `Traffic.cs`, `Walking.cs`, `Actors.cs` and the `ExploreView.cs` obstacle list.

### Cited Findings
- Model basis: SUMO crossing-blocking rule and `jmCrossingGap` 10 m; pedestrian whole-crossing-clear rule; impatience growth over 120 s (pedestrians) or 180 s (drivers); jam timeouts — [SUMO Pedestrians](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Simulation/Pedestrians.md), [SUMO vehicle types](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Definition_of_Vehicles%2C_Vehicle_Types%2C_and_Routes.md)
- SUMO places crossings at junctions automatically (`--crossings.guess`), and splits an edge with an extra junction if a mid-block crossing is wanted — [SUMO Pedestrians](https://github.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/blob/main/docs/web/docs/Simulation/Pedestrians.md)
- Yielding is higher at marked or junction crossings than at unmarked mid-block sites — [FHWA-HRT-13-098](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/13098/001.cfm)

### Inferences (implementation plan)
1. **API change:** `Traffic.Update(dt, obstacles, walkers)`, where `walkers` is a list of `struct WalkerIntent { Vec2 pos; Vec2 vel; int crossingId; byte phase; }` built from the townsfolk in `ExploreView` (~L1902), or better, from the shared sim, so it stays engine-free. Townsfolk *on the sidewalk* are ignored by cars. Only `Waiting` (kerb, for yield decisions) and `Crossing` walkers matter.
2. **Car longitudinal model:** in `Update`, compute `sLead = GapAhead − carLen`, `sStop = min over (committed/occupied crossings on the path ahead, path-swept obstacles) − buffer`, then `acc = IDM(v, min(sLead, sStop), Δv)`. Replace `car.speed += (target − speed)·3dt` with a ballistic update. Keep the U-turn speed cap. Count `car.wait` only when the blocker is a static obstacle (player, parked car), not a crossing.
3. **Crossings as data:** at layout time, create a `Crossing { road, sAlong, width, kind: Junction|MidBlock }` at each `net.crossings` junction-mouth link, at about 0.5–0.8 tile back from the junction node so cars stop before the corner curve (`CORNER = 0.9`). For mid-block `CrossStreet`, create a transient crossing at the walker's `ArcPosition` on the road, deleted when done. Optionally restrict mid-block crossings to straight segments at least 3 tiles from a junction, with visibility.
4. **Walker logic in `SidewalkWalk`/`Pedestrian`:** `CrossStreet()` and the junction branch of `AtEnd()` start `Approach` → `Look` → `Wait` instead of setting `crossing` immediately. `IsCrossing` only becomes true after commit. The gap test needs a traffic query, `Traffic.TimeToArrival(road, sAlong, side)`, returning the min tta over approaching cars in both lanes (with a yielding flag). Add a group-start rule and an impatience margin (1.5 s shrinking to 0.5 s over about 15 s). Lower the mid-block frequency of `dt·0.02` slightly, or bias it toward quiet roads.
5. **Car yielding:** for a walker `Waiting` at a crossing within the car's look-ahead and outside its braking distance, roll once: `pYield` ≈ 0.85 at a junction, ≈ 0.4 mid-block. Main roads (2 tiles) get slightly lower yield than streets, matching "yielding falls as speed rises". On yes, add a virtual leader and set `yieldingTo`.
6. **Commitment and deadlock:** a car inside `v²/(2b) + 0.3` of an uncommitted crossing becomes committed, and walkers must wait. A car waiting more than 6 s takes the priority token. The walker jam timeout lets a walker stuck mid-road for more than 3 s just keep walking. Never U-turn for walkers.
7. **Sensor fix:** make `BlockedAhead` sample the future lane, Corner and U-turn path rather than projecting along `car.heading`. Look-ahead distance is `max(2.5·GAP, v·T + v²/(2b) + s0)`.
8. **Polish:** walkers face traffic while waiting, waves and headlight flashes, a honk after 3 s, a startle hop on near misses, and hurried pace when crossing in front of a waiting car. All are driven by phase or event flags synced at 10 Hz.
9. **Tests (pure C#):** deterministic replay (same seed gives identical poses after N s, with walkers crossing); an invariant that no car centre comes within 0.4 tiles of a crossing walker; no deadlock (every waiting walker crosses within 30 s and every car passes a crossing within 15 s in a dense seed).
